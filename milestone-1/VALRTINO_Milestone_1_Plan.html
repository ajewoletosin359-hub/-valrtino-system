4.1 Overview

↔
Agentsn8n workflows using the Claude API. One workflow group per agent.
↔
External servicesShopify, Meta, TikTok, supplier, Claude API, speech services.

Everything except the external services runs on one VPS in your account, started with Docker Compose. The server can be moved to another provider by copying the repository, the database backup and the configuration file.

4.2 Why n8n together with custom code

  • n8n for the agents. Each agent's steps are visible as a diagram, can be edited without a programmer for simple changes, and can be exported as files. This keeps the agents easy to maintain and replace.
  • Custom code for control. Login, approvals, the audit log, business rules, the STOP switch and the reports are safety-critical. They are written as tested code so that an agent cannot bypass them.

4.3 Approval flow

RECOMMENDED → WAITING FOR APPROVAL → APPROVED / REJECTED → EXECUTED → RESULT

  1. An agent proposes an action. It is saved with its reason and the data used.
  2. The dashboard shows it in the approval queue and sends you an alert.
  3. You approve or reject. The decision is recorded with your user and the time.
  4. Only an approved action receives a one-time execution permission. The agent must present it to act, and it cannot be used twice.
  5. The outcome is written back as the result and feeds the agent's history.

Agents hold no ability to act on their own. Every external change passes through this gate, and the audit log shows what was recommended, what you approved and what was executed.

4.4 Modularity

Each agent is a separate unit made of: its n8n workflows, its prompts, its settings, and one entry in the agent list in the database. Agents talk to the rest of the system only through the dashboard's interface, never directly to each other. To add an agent, a developer adds a new entry and its workflows. To replace or switch one off, its entry is changed. The other agents continue to run. The handover documentation includes a step-by-step guide named "How to add a new agent".

4.6 Business rules, version history and the STOP switch

  • Business rules are settings, not code. Minimum profit and margin, price range, maximum shipping time, target countries, supplier requirements, saturation limits, refund and complaint thresholds, advertising thresholds, scaling rules and Stop-Loss rules are edited in the dashboard.
  • Version history. Every change to a prompt, scoring rule, business rule or agent setting creates a new version. A previous version can be restored from the dashboard. n8n workflows are exported to your repository at every milestone, which gives them version history too.
  • Global STOP. One control in the dashboard stops all automated actions: publishing, Meta and TikTok actions, budget changes, scaling, refunds, customer-service actions, orders and all workflows. While it is on, even approved actions do not execute.

4.7 Security

  • No password or API key is written in the code. Secrets are held in a protected configuration file on your server and in n8n's encrypted credential store.
  • The dashboard is reached only over HTTPS, with login required, hashed passwords and separate roles for owner and developer.
  • The firewall allows only web traffic and key-based server access. The database is not reachable from the internet.
  • Spending and publishing are protected twice: by the approval gate (4.3) and by the limits you set per market and per test.
  • Every login, approval and execution is logged.

4.9 Error handling and duplicate protection

  • Calls to Shopify, Meta, TikTok, the supplier and the AI service are retried with increasing delays, respect each service's rate limits, and stop after a time limit.
  • Failed actions are logged, shown in the dashboard and sent as alerts. A failure never causes an unapproved action.
  • Each approved action carries a unique reference and executes once only. Products, campaigns and supplier orders are checked against existing records before creation, so the same item is not created twice.
  • If an external service is down, the action waits in a queue and is retried, or is returned to you as failed.

4.11 Product scoring formula

Each factor is scored from 0 to 100, multiplied by its weight, and the results are added to give a final score from 0 to 100. The weights below are the proposed starting values. They are visible and editable in the dashboard.

FactorWeightFactorWeight
Profit and margin (cost, shipping cost, selling price)20Supplier quality and reviews10
Demand and trend strength15Refund and return risk, including sizing10
Competition and saturation10Product reviews5
Advertising potential10Complaint history5
Shipping time10Historical performance in your store5

Before scoring, a product is rejected outright if it fails a hard rule: IP-risk flag, below minimum margin, above maximum shipping time, outside your price range, or not available for your target markets. Every score shows its factor breakdown, its data sources with dates, and whether it is an estimate or proven by a test. An untested product is never labelled a winner.

4.12 Net profit formula

Estimated net profit = Revenue − product cost − supplier shipping − advertising spend − payment processing fees − Shopify and app fees where attributable − discounts − refunds and cancellations − other agreed variable costs
Cost or valueData source
Revenue, discounts, refunds, cancellationsShopify orders (confirmed values)
Product cost and supplier shippingSupplier order data once the supplier is connected in Milestone 5. Until then, the cost recorded per product.
Advertising spendMeta and TikTok reporting
Payment processing feesYour PayPlus and PayPal rates, entered as settings. Shown as estimates.
Shopify and app feesYour plan rates, entered as settings. Shown as estimates.
Other variable costsSettings you define.

Profit is calculated per order, product, campaign and market. Each figure is labelled confirmed or estimate. Scaling recommendations use profit, not ROAS alone.

4.13 Continuous learning and its safety rules

ACTION → RESULT → ANALYSIS → LEARNING → IMPROVED NEXT DECISION

Every agent stores what it did, why, the data used, the result, whether it succeeded, what was learned and what should change. These records adjust product scores, creative choices, test recommendations, CRO recommendations and scaling advice. Agents can propose changes to prompts, scoring weights and rules. They cannot change code or infrastructure.

Safety ruleProposed starting value (editable)
Minimum data before a weight changes10 completed product tests, or 50 orders, relevant to that factor
Maximum change at one time5 points per weight, at most once a week
ApprovalEvery proposed change to a weight, rule or prompt needs your approval
How a change is evaluatedResults of the next 10 tests compared with the previous 10. Controlled comparison where possible; otherwise labelled as a before-and-after observation.
Detecting a poor changeIf results are worse after the change, the system flags it and recommends restoring the previous version
Return to a previous configurationOne action in the dashboard restores any earlier version (4.6)
Insufficient evidenceReported as "not enough data" with no recommendation forced
